# The shop window moves into the AI assistant

**Standfirst:** Zillow put its listings inside Claude, Optimove gave AI tools access to approved brand material, and Demandbase launched an agent that measures what AI assistants say about a brand. Meanwhile vendors started billing AI work in very different ways.

**Period:** 19–25 October 2026

> **In brief**
> - On 19 October Zillow launched a Claude connector where Zillow controls the display and routes requests back to its own channels.
> - Optimove launched Content Studio, giving AI tools approved assets and brand guidelines and checking campaigns before they go out.
> - On 20 October Demandbase launched an AEO agent that compares how AI assistants describe a company.
> - On 20 October Salesforce agreed to acquire Braze for about $5.66 billion.
> - Kochava, ThoughtSpot and Peec AI launched three different ways to bill AI work: token budgets, payment per completed task, and credits.

## Zillow shows the way in Claude

On 19 October Zillow put its listings, home values and calculators inside Claude through a connector where a buyer can request a tour, contact an agent or explore financing without leaving the conversation. Zillow says no data is sent to Anthropic and the display carries the same attribution as on Zillow.com.

The interesting part is the terms: Anthropic runs the interface, but Zillow controls what appears and where the lead goes. Other brands planning AI connectors should ask for the same.

## The brand book becomes a data source

The same day Optimove launched Content Studio, giving its own AI agents – and outside tools such as Claude and ChatGPT – access to approved assets, brand voice and guidelines. Two agents check campaigns for brand alignment, broken links, missing images and wrong promotional details before they are sent.

It is the same shift as in week 41, when Meta tested importing brand guidelines: the brand book stops being a document and becomes something machines read and act on.

## What does AI say about you?

On 20 October Demandbase launched an AEO (answer engine optimisation) agent that compares how major AI assistants describe and recommend a company and identifies the sources behind those answers. Demandbase cites a Gartner prediction that AI agents will intermediate 90% of B2B buying by 2028.

The same week Peec AI expanded its tool with an agent that traces drops in AI-answer visibility and proposes fixes – and by default asks for approval before changing anything.

## Salesforce buys Braze

On 20 October Salesforce agreed to acquire Braze for about $5.66 billion in enterprise value, or $47.50 in cash per share. Braze serves around 2,770 customers, and the deal is expected to close in Salesforce's fiscal year 2028.

It is the week's biggest marketing news. For companies using Braze without being Salesforce customers, the counterparty in their contract will change. Worth reviewing the terms now.

## What does the meter count?

On 22 October three vendors showed three ways to bill AI work. Kochava added token budgets per user and team and routes each request to the cheapest adequate model. ThoughtSpot launched AgentSpot, charging for neither tokens nor seats but per completed conversation, workflow or app session. Peec AI uses a shared monthly credit pool.

None has published prices that allow comparison. But the question is worth asking at your next purchase: are you paying for usage, for users or for results?

## FAQ

### What is AEO?

Answer engine optimisation: making sure AI assistants describe and recommend your company correctly. Like search engine optimisation, but for answers instead of search results.

### Should we build a Claude or ChatGPT connector like Zillow?

Only if your customers actually start buying there. If you do, insist that you control the display and that leads come to you.

### What does Salesforce buying Braze mean for us?

If you use Braze, review your contract terms for change of ownership, pricing and data integration before the deal closes.
