## Two conferences, one message

Week 38 was martech's big week. Dreamforce 2026 ran 15–17 September at the Moscone Center in San Francisco with more than 1,600 breakout sessions organised around the "Agentic Enterprise" theme. In parallel, UNBOUND 2026 — HubSpot's renamed INBOUND — ran 16–18 September in Boston with more than 200 sessions aimed at go-to-market teams across marketing, sales, service and ops.

The message was near-identical at both: agents, not assistants. The shift matters rhetorically. An assistant suggests a subject line; an agent updates the segment, writes the variant, buys the placement and reports back. It is the same distinction Anthropic put numbers on that week in its own R&D — assistance, collaboration, leadership.

For marketing leaders this means vendor roadmaps now assume you have your data foundation, rights and approval flows in order. Agents acting on bad data act quickly on bad data.

## The money moves toward AI search

Around 15–16 September, Mediaocean launched a new unit, Mediaocean AI Ventures, to invest in advertising AI startups. It signals where capital expects the value: not in yet another creative tool, but in the infrastructure between brand, agency and ad platform.

Profound presented its AI Marketer, which deploys sub-agents to refresh stale content, find earned media openings and launch ads, plus an Ads Studio that builds AI search campaigns across the OpenAI, Google and Meta ad managers. The company says it serves more than 1,000 enterprise brands, including more than a third of the Fortune 100.

The category is new enough that measurement is immature. But the direction is clear: visibility inside AI answers is being treated as a buyable channel, not just an SEO byproduct.

## Google closes more controls

From 1 September, Google began automatically migrating campaign-level Broad Match campaigns and legacy Automatically Created Assets to AI Max. The rollout is gradual through the month, so many advertisers hit it during week 38. Google says AI Max for Search delivers on average 7% more conversions or conversion value at a similar CPA or ROAS when the full feature suite is used.

Less discussed but more consequential for B2B: Google has introduced a 7-day limit on offline conversion uploads. Conversions uploaded more than seven days after they occur are ignored in data-driven attribution.

For companies with long sales cycles — where a lead becomes an order after weeks — that is a real change in what the platform can learn from. If your CRM export runs monthly, it is now effectively invisible.

## Meta does the same from the other side

Meta is removing the ability to manually control certain placements at ad set level, placing ads instead where it expects to deliver the selected objective. In exchange, advertisers can now build custom audiences specifically for exclusion — existing customers, people who opted out, and other groups they do not want to reach.

At the same time, Meta AI can now connect to sources including Facebook and Instagram analytics, Meta Ads and Google Workspace to produce insights and recommendations across assets.

The pattern across both platforms is the same: less manual control over *where* and *who*, more control through *signals* and *exclusions*. The skill moves from campaign setup to data quality.

## The maturity gap is still the real problem

A study by StackAdapt and Ascend2 finds that only 39% of agencies have significantly integrated AI into day-to-day workflows, while 18% have barely started.

Hold that against the week's conference rhetoric. Vendors are selling agents to organisations where fewer than four in ten have basic AI in the workflow. The gap is not technological — the tools work — but organisational: who approves, who corrects, who is accountable when an agent buys the wrong thing.

The practical recommendation from week 38: pick one high-frequency, low-risk process — ad copy variants, product feed enrichment, report summarisation — and take it all the way to production with a named owner before buying an agent platform.

## FAQ

### Should we accept the automatic AI Max migration?

For most accounts resisting costs more than adapting, but do not migrate blind. Watch search term reports closely for the first four weeks, and set up negative keywords and brand exclusions before the campaign moves — AI Max expands matching more broadly than classic Broad Match.

### What do we do about the 7-day offline conversion limit?

Automate the export from CRM to Google Ads so it runs daily, or at least twice a week. If your real sales cycle is longer than seven days, define an earlier qualified milestone as the conversion — "meeting booked", for instance — rather than the final order.

### Is AI search optimisation worth budget now?

As an experiment, not as a channel shift. Measurement is immature and the traffic share is still small for most companies. Start by measuring how often you are mentioned at all in AI answers for your 20 most important queries, before buying a tool.
