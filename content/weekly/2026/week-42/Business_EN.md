## Inference is a billion-dollar business

The week's largest investment went to Modal Labs, which provides cloud infrastructure for running AI models. It raised $750 million in a Series D led by Accel at a $15.75 billion valuation and says it recently passed a $1 billion annual revenue run rate.

Note what the money is for. Not training new models, but running them. As models get cheaper per token, as in week 41, usage rises – and demand shifts to the infrastructure that delivers the answers.

## Fewer mega-rounds, same direction

According to Crunchbase, investors scaled back the largest rounds this week, with only two financings in the hundreds of millions. After Modal Labs came Icon, which builds robotic construction systems, with $200 million.

Among AI companies, Labelbox, which builds tools to train and evaluate models, raised $100 million in a Series E. Suki, which turns clinical conversations into documentation and coding, raised $90 million.

The pattern of recent weeks holds: capital flows to companies providing infrastructure or automating a specific workflow with a measurable outcome.

## The price of a frontier model

On 13 October Google launched Gemini 4 Argon for all paying developers at an introductory price of $2 per million input tokens and $10 per million output tokens, with cached input tokens 95% off.

"Introductory" is the key word. The price can change, and a business case that only works at the introductory price is fragile. Together with the week's open models – Beam and Muse Spark Open – organisations now have more real alternatives, and that brings negotiating power.

## Analytics moves from dashboard to agent

On 12 October Salesforce agreed to acquire Loops, an AI platform that monitors key metrics, explains why they change and suggests where to act. Loops will become part of Tableau. Terms were not disclosed.

For leadership it signals how business reporting is changing: from someone remembering to open the dashboard, to the system reporting deviations itself and explaining why.

## Three questions for the next budget

**What does it cost to run your AI, not just to buy it?** Inference costs grow with use. Know the number per task.

**How dependent are you on one vendor's introductory price?** Model what happens if the price rises, and have an alternative.

**Which reports could become alerts?** Find the three metrics where you would most want to know the same day if they change.

## FAQ

### Why is so much invested in inference?

Because usage rises as the price per token falls. The more tasks run on AI, the greater the need for infrastructure that delivers answers quickly and cheaply.

### Should we wait for prices to fall before choosing a model?

No, but avoid locking yourself to one price. Build so you can switch models without rebuilding everything.

### What does Salesforce buying Loops mean for us?

If you use Tableau, expect features that explain changes in your numbers automatically. Whatever your platform, it is a good time to decide which metrics deserve an alert.
