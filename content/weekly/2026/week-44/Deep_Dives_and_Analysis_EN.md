# You don't get the strongest model – yet

**Standfirst:** In week 44 both Google and OpenAI kept their most advanced models reserved for selected users. The reason is the same: the capabilities can serve defence and attack alike. That changes how new models reach organisations.

**Period:** 26 October–1 November 2026

> **In brief**
> - Google gives Gemini 4 Carbon to selected cyber defenders first and expands gradually.
> - OpenAI gives GPT-6 Rosalind Research for life sciences only to approved research organisations.
> - OpenAI's own system card rates the broadly available GPT-6.1 Terra "Critical" for cybersecurity.
> - For organisations, "newest model" no longer means "available model".

## Three models, three access levels

Gemini 4 Carbon is, according to Google, its strongest model, with leading results in finding, exploiting and patching software vulnerabilities. Google initially gives it to a selected group of cyber defenders and expands gradually to trusted partners while refining guardrails. Google is also taking part in the US government's voluntary pre-release model testing.

GPT-6 Rosalind Research is OpenAI's model for life-sciences research. Access is limited to approved research organisations, with safeguards designed for biological work.

GPT-6.1 Terra, by contrast, is broadly available at a low price – even though OpenAI's own system card, according to Jisc, rates it "Critical" for cybersecurity capability.

## Why dual use changes the rollout

A model that is good at finding vulnerabilities is good at it whether the user wants to close them or exploit them. The same applies to models for biological research. This is called dual use, and it is why vendors now differentiate access.

It is a change from recent years, when the newest model typically launched to all paying customers on the same day. Now the order is: selected defenders and researchers first, then partners, and finally the broad customer base.

## Defence comes first

The logic is to give defenders a head start. If a model finds vulnerabilities faster than people, it is better that those who can close them get it first.

That this is not theoretical was shown in October: Jisc reports that PHP vulnerabilities found by researchers using OpenAI and Google models, after more than a decade undetected, are now being broadly exploited, and that Amazon saw an exploit attempt four days before public disclosure.

## What it means for you

**Do not plan around the announcement date.** The model in an announcement is not necessarily the one you can buy.

**Keep your systems patched.** When AI makes finding vulnerabilities faster, the time from discovery to attack shrinks. Patching becomes more urgent, not less.

**Ask your security vendor.** Do they have access to the new defensive models, and how do they use them?

## FAQ

### Is limiting access good or bad?

It is a trade-off. It gives defenders a head start, but also means the strongest tools reach ordinary organisations later.

### Is GPT-6.1 Terra dangerous to use?

The "Critical" rating concerns what the model could potentially be used for, not that ordinary use is unsafe. It does underline that broadly available models also have strong capabilities that require responsible use.

### What should an organisation do?

Prioritise patching, and ask your security vendors how they use AI to find and close vulnerabilities.
