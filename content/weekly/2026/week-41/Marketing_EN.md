## Measurement arrives for AI ads

On 5 October, DoubleVerify added attribution measurement for ChatGPT Ads. It sounds technical, but it is the kind of news that decides whether a channel becomes real.

An ad channel without independent measurement is an experiment budget. An ad channel with attribution and verification from a third party that media agencies already use is a budget line. The shift from the first to the second is usually what releases the actual money.

It is still early, and it does not change the fact that traffic share from AI answers is modest for most companies. But the infrastructure is being built now, and it is being built by the same vendors that already measure the rest of the media buy.

## Agent checkout meets reality

The same week, InMobi published a figure that puts a number on how far retail has come: 36% of US storefronts are ready to handle a purchase completed by an AI agent.

That number deserves to be read both ways. A good third is already ready — faster than you might expect. And nearly two thirds are not, which means an agent attempting a purchase fails more often than it succeeds.

During the week Constructor launched agentic checkout powered by Stripe, letting a shopper move from product discovery to purchase inside a single agent experience. The combination of the two items is the interesting part: the tools for selling to agents now exist, but the substrate is only half built.

For a retailer the practical question is not whether to "bet on agentic commerce". It is whether product data, stock status and payment flow are structured enough for a machine to complete a purchase without guessing.

## Tools that address agents

On 8 October two launches pointed the same way. Growth7 shipped an AI marketing platform that turns CRM data into concrete pipeline actions. MediaRadar launched Insights Studio, an ad sales intelligence platform that the company says serves both revenue teams and AI agents.

That phrasing — "and AI agents" — is appearing in product descriptions often enough now to take seriously. Vendors are no longer designing only a user interface. They are also designing an interface another company's agent can use.

The consequence for a marketing organisation is that your data soon has two audiences: the employee reading the dashboard, and the machine fetching the numbers.

## Brand guidelines become a machine-readable asset

Meta is testing a Business Assistant where advertisers can import their brand guidelines and then change campaigns through a conversation.

That moves something that has been a PDF in a shared folder into a system that acts on it. If the guidelines are vague — "our tone is professional but warm" — the machine produces vague work. If they are concrete, with examples of approved and rejected phrasing, it has something to work with.

This is probably the year's most underrated marketing task: rewriting the brand book from something a human interprets into something a model can follow.

## What to do next sprint

Three tasks with short lead time and visible effect:

**Check whether your storefront can complete an agent purchase.** Try it yourself with one of the available tools. The result is usually more instructive than a report.

**Measure how often you are mentioned in AI answers for your 20 most important queries.** That is still the baseline everything else is judged against.

**Rewrite one section of the brand book as examples.** Take tone or product descriptions and replace adjectives with approved and rejected phrasings. That is the format machines can use.

## FAQ

### Should we put budget into ChatGPT Ads now?

As a measurable test, not as a channel shift. Attribution measurement has only just arrived, so for the first time you can see what the money does. Start with an amount you can afford to learn from, and hold it against your existing paid search.

### How do we know whether our storefront is ready for agent checkout?

Try completing a purchase with an agent. The usual stopping points are CAPTCHA, mandatory account creation, unclear variants, and stock status that is not machine-readable. That is the list to work down.

### Isn't it too early to rewrite the brand book?

No, because the work pays off regardless. A brand book with concrete examples of approved and rejected phrasing also makes new staff and agencies faster. Machines being able to use it is a bonus, not the premise.
